session-open calls worker-placement for a worker, reattach-handshake for the data path's first handshake and typing-ownership for who holds the keyboard.
byte-relay carries the bytes once reattach-handshake reaches `live`. A surface attaches afterwards through session-attach.

**Words used here.**

- **Connection.** One authenticated transport to one target, inside one worker process, which holds N of them
  (Contract §15). A telnet connection is one TCP connection with no authentication of its own (RFC 854): its one
  login is typed by the operator in its one session. **Session.** One channel opened on a connection: what is recorded, attached to and ended
  (Contract §15). Here it is always a `shell`: a `session` channel with `pty-req` and `shell` (common `session_type`).
- **Candidate.** A connection `connection: reuse` may take: this principal's own, to this target, with the same
  credential, transport up, with or without a session on it (`SessionCreateRequest`).
- **Lingering connection.** A connection with no session left, kept up for `connection_linger` so that a quick
  reopen reuses the login (session model §5, §11).
- **Host key, fingerprint.** The key an SSH server proves itself with, and its digest in OpenSSH `SHA256:` form
  (common `fingerprint`). **First use:** the first time the worker meets a target's key. On a connect that is not in
  prompt mode it accepts, warns and records it; in prompt mode the worker asks first (`hostkey_ask`) and records the key
  only when the operator accepts (device-login steps 11 to 13). A different key later is refused (`HostKey`; `hostkey`).
- **`known_hosts`.** The worker's store of recorded host keys, in its own volume. It has no contract shape
  (system-architecture "State and where it lives").
- **Supplied login.** A username and password for one new connection, carried in `SessionCreateRequest.login` and used
  only to authenticate that connection. The create stores nothing of it (V10; a user who keeps a login saves it into their own vault by a separate store request of their own, C7, which is the vault's), and it is the operator's explicit choice of credential
  (Contract §12; conventions V14). **Stored credential:** the one the core resolves for the principal and target
  (step 3): from the vault release a vault entry, named by its reference and its layer (`personal` through the
  principal's Layer 2a mapping, `org` through a grant). A request with a login resolves none.
- **Device login (prompt mode).** A login whose `auth.kind` is `prompt`: the create carries only the account, answers 202 and opens
  nothing; the device's own SSH login questions are answered in the pane before any session exists (device-login). It is
  not a supplied login: no secret is on the request. **Prompted connection:** one a device login opened; its record
  keeps `credential_ref` `supplied` and carries `login: prompted` (step 18), and its principal may reuse it by naming its
  `connection_id`, or by a reuse that names none, as one of its candidates (steps 3 and 4).
- **Escrow.** The control token held by the core while no attached client may hold it (PTY §7.1).

## Path

new connection: SF-CO → CO-FS → (worker-placement) → CO-PW → PW-TG → PW-FS → CO-PW → PW-TG → CO-PW → PW-FS → CO-VT → (reattach-handshake)
→ CO-BB → CO-DK → (typing-ownership) → SF-CO

reuse: SF-CO → CO-FS → CO-PW → PW-TG → CO-PW → PW-FS → CO-VT → (reattach-handshake) → CO-BB → CO-DK → (typing-ownership) → SF-CO

## Refusals and who is told

- **The choices the broker presents.** Two or more credentials, or two or more candidate connections, are answered
  409 and never picked (Contract §12; conventions V14). After `connection_choice_required` a surface MUST let the
  operator pick one of `connection_ids` and repeat the request with `connection_id` (error `connection_ids`)
  [owed]. A surface MUST NOT describe reuse or a new connection as isolation: both land in a worker that holds N
  connections and die with it (Contract §15.3) [owed].
- **Telnet and serial targets**: telnet connects from this release; its create sends no credential. Serial is
  answered 502 `target_connect_failed` until a later release connects it (CHANGELOG contract-v0.2, known gaps;
  ruling Q4 (b)) [C1/session-create-serial-502, reference, owed by the E3 core plan]. A create on a telnet target
  with no supervisor at `proto_version` 0.5 or later is 503 `worker_unavailable`, and no worker is asked for
  [C1/session-create-needs-v0.5-503, reference, owed by the E3 core plan].
- **The credential (from the vault release).** `credential_choice_required` carries its candidates; a choice that is
  not a candidate, or one made beside a `login`, is 422; a new connection whose personal credential needs an unlock this
  login does not hold is 409 `vault_locked`, and the surface unlocks (→ vault-unlock) and repeats the create; a new
  connection whose `user_unlocked` org credential's storing administrator holds no unlock on any login is 409
  `org_credential_locked`, and the surface says that the administrator must unlock and never asks for the operator's
  passphrase, which cannot help; under the
  revocation switch a login tied to a revoked device is 403 `device_not_trusted`; a Layer 2a mapping to a reference the vault no longer holds is 409 `mapped_credential_missing`, naming it (step 3). Each is decided before any
  worker is asked, so a refused create leaves nothing behind.
- **A supplied login.** A `login` on `reuse` and on a target that is not `ssh` are 422 `invalid_request`; one the
  target refuses is 502 `target_auth_failed`; none of them echoes a value of the request, and no refusal of a request
  with a login is 409 `no_resolving_credential` or `credential_choice_required` (steps 1, 2a, 3, 11). A login that
  would be recorded and does not set `recording_acknowledged` is 409 `login_recording_not_acknowledged`: nothing was
  sent to a worker or recorded, and the surface warns and repeats the request with the field set (steps 2b, 22).
- **A device login (Release B).** A prompt-mode create is refused 403 `forbidden` when the principal may not join a
  session on the target, 429 `rate_limited` past `worker.device_logins_max`, and 503 `worker_unavailable` when no worker
  at `proto_version` 0.7 or later can be placed, each before anything is placed; an unacknowledged recording is 409 as
  for any login; none echoes a value of the request (device-login steps 3 to 6).
- **An unknown code.** A consumer handles an error code it does not know by its HTTP status (V4) [owed].
- **Which answer wins** when a visible target fails both `session.create` and credential resolution, 403 or 409, is
  not stated: `createSession` describes each alone (Open items).
- **Who is told.** Only the caller, in every case. A refused create writes no audit event: the vocabulary has none
  for it (`audit-event.schema.json` top), with two exceptions: a supplied login the target refuses is audited by
  `target.auth_failed` (step 19), and so is a device login that ended after an answered attempt; and a first-use host key
  the operator refuses in a device login is audited by `target.hostkey_refused` (step 19). A created session is audited by `session.connect` (step 19).
- **No host detail** in any answer: an error `message` names no broker path, pid or connection directory (error
  top; V11).

## Security considerations

- **The credential (Contract §4; open_decisions §5).** It crosses edge CO-PW only inside `connect.auth` (and, from 0.7, a device login's answers inside `auth_answer`), on the new
  connection's own control socket. It never goes into `create_worker`, argv, the environment, a manifest, the
  spool or a data socket (ipc-control top). The control socket and `create_worker` cases are graded
  [C3/control-message-shape; C3/supervisor-request-shape; C5/manifest-closed-v0.1]; argv and the environment are
  owed. The worker injects it below the recorded byte stream, so it is never in the PTY stream or a recording
  (step 8). Durable state holds `credential_ref`, a reference only (step 18). `supplied` is reserved: no stored
  credential, now or in a later release's vault, may use it as its reference. A reuse sends no credential at all:
  it shares the connection's authentication (Contract §15.3), and the vault is touched only when a connection is
  established (credential §10.2).
- **A supplied login (open_decisions §5; credential §10.1).** It crosses SF-CO once, in the body of the create request,
  and CO-PW once, in `connect.auth`, as a stored password does (a device login has no password on the create: its answers
  cross in C2 `device_login_answer` and C3 `auth_answer`, device-login Security considerations); it goes into SSH's authentication at the protocol layer,
  below the byte stream, so it is not typed input and is not in the PTY stream. The core MUST NOT write it to a log line,
  an error message, an audit event, bbolt, a manifest, the spool or a data socket, and MUST NOT keep it past `connect` (with the knob off) or past the write of the recording's head (with it on:
  it is held until then only to be recorded) [owed: C1/session-create-login-never-echoed grades the answers]. It is
  recorded only when `policy.yaml` `recording.login_input` is true, marked as login input (C8 codes 6 and 7), and the
  audit says so per session (`detail.login_recorded`). A language may not be able to wipe a string, so "drops" means that no reference is kept
  and nothing is copied, and the contract claims no more. A core older than this release refuses any `login` with 422, because its request shapes are
  closed: it never silently uses a stored credential in place of a login it was given. A login lets any holder of
  `session.create` try a visible target's passwords through TSB, which is why a refused one is audited (step 19); no
  further permission gates it, and a rate limit is a later item.
- **A device login (Release B).** The create carries no secret. The answers cross only C2 `device_login_answer` and C3
  `auth_answer`, never a data socket, the ring, the VT grid, a log, a manifest or the spool, and are recorded only when
  `recording.login_input` is on and consent was given; the create, the device login and the core store nothing of a
  login (V10). Remember is the user's own separate store into their vault, never the create's. Full text: device-login
  Security considerations.
- **A telnet `connect` carries no credential**: TSB types no vault secret into a stream. What the operator types (a
  telnet login, an enable secret) is session input, recorded verbatim (open_decisions §5).
- **Host keys.** Accepted on first use, recorded, reported to the creator in the 201 and audited
  (`target.hostkey_accepted`); a changed key is refused with no connection made (`HostKey`; `hostkey`; steps 9–11,
  19, 21). Only the creator is shown the fingerprint: `hostkey` exists in `SessionCreateResponse` alone, and no record or
  frame carries it, but `device_login_hostkey` (a device login's, in prompt mode, to the creator's device-login socket
  alone); the audit event keeps it. A refusal for a changed key writes no audit event (the vocabulary has
  none), so a possible interception attempt is not on the audit record.
- **The recording warning.** Whether a session is recorded is in the record, with fixed text that no surface can
  soften (session-record `recording`); a surface shows it before attaching or typing (→ session-attach rule CR1). The recording's
  path never reaches a surface: `recording_path` is bbolt and audit data only (continuity-state `recording_path`;
  Storage §6), and the record is closed [C1/session-record-shape].
- **The vault (from the vault release).** The core never returns a decrypted credential; at each establishment it opens
  a personal credential from the principal's set in bbolt with this login's unlock, and a `user_unlocked` org credential
  with its storing administrator's (credential §8.2), and drops the plaintext once `connect.auth` is encoded (DEFAULT 33).
  The login name the target receives is the credential's (credential §10.3). A credential choice lists references and
  layers only. An establishment is the only time the vault is read (credential §10.2).
- **Tenants (Contract §4).** `connection_ids` lists the requesting principal's own connections only
  [C1/reuse-409-lists-candidates]. An invisible target reads as an absent one (step 2).

## Open items

- **A supplied-login connection and reuse by id.** Whether a connection opened with a supplied login becomes reusable by
  `connection_id`, as a prompted one is (step 4), is open; at this release it stays never reusable.
- **G-5 (wording only).** The creation size is clamped and the 201 carries the applied size (→ size-control steps 15–18);
  the contract text does not say so yet (verifier report G-5).
- **G-11.** What the surface does when the operator declines a first-use host key, with the session already live
  (plan §7 G-11; step 22).
- **G-12, closed by the vault release** (verifier): the choice is `credential_ref` with `credential_layer` on the
  create (step 3).
- **Not in plan §7:**
  - the HTTP answer, and whether the core closes what it opened, when `open_shell` is answered `error`, the
    connection is lost before `channel_opened`, or the handshake does not reach `live` (steps 14, 17). Error
    `x-codes` gives 502 `target_connect_failed` to "the connection failed before a session opened"; whether "opened"
    means `channel_opened` or `live` is not said. One late case is settled: a `channel_opened` that arrives after the
    core's own wait expired is closed with `close_channel` and, on its `channel_closed`, released, with nothing
    recorded; a late `error` drops the open (step 14);
  - which C3 event reports a target that cannot be reached (502 `target_connect_failed`), and what HTTP answer a
    `connect` refused `bad_request`, `not_supported` or `internal` gets (step 11);
  - what the worker does with a connection refused by `auth_failed` or `hostkey_changed`: its directory, and its
    own exit when it holds no other connection (step 11);
  - where the bbolt writes fall among steps 11–17; only "before `session.connect`" is fixed (step 18);
  - what session-open does when no VT worker answers at creation (step 16; E2);
  - no knob bounds the core's wait for `connected`. Its wait for `channel_opened` is bounded from this release: the
    target's `open_timeout` plus one `heartbeat_interval`, toward every worker, with or without `open_timeout`
    (step 12);
  - 403 or 409 when a principal lacks `session.create` and no credential resolves (step 2–3);
  - whether `GET /sessions` may list a session before its handshake reaches `live` (`live` says "offered
    northbound", naming the 201 and attach only).
- **OPEN — approval items leaned on.** D15 (the VT socket directory) and D17 (the C4 message names, `open`) carry
  step 16; both are open southbound items (approval "Southbound items"; queue A8). Not ruled.
- **Reusing a connection opened with a supplied login.** At this release such a connection is never a reuse candidate
  (step 4): every create with a login is a new connection. A later release may let the same principal reuse it by naming
  its `connection_id`, or list every live connection of the principal to the target as candidates, which would change
  step 3's order (today a reuse with no stored credential is refused before candidates are looked at).
- **Known gap (CHANGELOG contract-v0.1 and v0.2).** A target whose host key changes legitimately is refused
  `hostkey_changed`, and no API accepts the new key. An administrator's acceptance of a changed key, and a declined
  first-use key, are one item after E4, each needing a worker message and a C3 change (Q10 (i), (iii)).
